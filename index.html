Research Interests

Contactless Human Sensing and Physiological State Modeling

My research centers on contactless human sensing and physiological state modeling. By combining wireless sensing, environmental sensing, and machine learning, I study the relationship between environmental changes and human physiological responses and explore ways to monitor human states with less reliance on cameras and wearable devices. I am particularly interested in individual differences in physiological responses, response delays, and the reliability of state estimates.

Wi-Fi CSI Environmental Sensing Physiological State Modeling Individual Differences Response Delay Reliability Evaluation Machine Learning

My current research focuses on three areas:

Wireless Sensing and Contactless Physiological Monitoring

I use Wi-Fi channel state information (CSI) to investigate sensing methods for respiration, activity, and related physiological changes, and evaluate their effectiveness against physiological measurements from wearable devices.

Modeling Environmental Changes and Physiological Responses

I combine environmental data, such as temperature and humidity, with physiological signals to analyze shared patterns, individual differences, and time delays in autonomic responses under the same environmental conditions, exploring associations between the environment and human states.

Multisource Information Fusion and Reliability Evaluation

I combine environmental information with human sensing data to explore methods for estimating physiological and affective states, using probabilistic modeling and uncertainty evaluation to improve the trustworthiness of the estimates.

In the future, I hope to apply these methods to everyday health monitoring, older adult care, and companion robots, developing sensing systems that balance privacy, user burden, and adaptability to real-world environments.
